Winter is hard on cars and drivers. Cold drains batteries, ice puts cars in ditches, and a closed highway can leave you sitting for hours. A small kit in the trunk makes a big difference. Here's what to pack, in priority order.

## The essentials

- [ ] **Jump starter or jumper cables.** A charged lithium jump starter (like the [NOCO Boost GB40](/gear/noco-boost-gb40/)) lets you start a dead battery without waiting for another car. Recharge it every couple of months, since cold drains it too.
- [ ] **Snow brush and ice scraper.** A [telescoping snow broom](/gear/snow-joe-telescoping-snow-broom/) reaches across an SUV or truck roof. Clear the whole car, roof included. Snow sliding off your roof can blind you or the driver behind you.
- [ ] **Phone charger cable and a charged power bank.**
- [ ] **Flashlight or headlamp** with spare batteries, such as the [Black Diamond Spot 400](/gear/black-diamond-spot-400/) or the [Wuben C2](/gear/wuben-c2/) flashlight. A headlamp keeps both hands free for tire changes.
- [ ] **Warm layers:** a blanket or [emergency bivvy](/gear/sol-emergency-bivvy/), plus a hat, gloves, and dry wool socks.
- [ ] **First aid kit.** See [what to put in a family first aid kit](/guides/storm-first-aid-kit/).
- [ ] **Water and high-calorie snacks** like granola bars, nuts, or jerky. Bottles can freeze, so leave some room in them.

## Helpful extras

- [ ] A small **folding shovel** to dig out around tires.
- [ ] **Traction aid:** a bag of sand or cat litter (non-clumping) or traction mats for when you're stuck on ice.
- [ ] **Multi-tool** for quick fixes, like the [Leatherman Wave+](/gear/leatherman-wave-plus/).
- [ ] **Hand warmers**, either single-use packs or rechargeable, like the [Zippo HeatBank 9s](/gear/zippo-heatbank-9s/).
- [ ] **Reflective triangles or flares** and a bright cloth to tie to your antenna.
- [ ] **Tow strap.**
- [ ] **Windshield washer fluid** rated for winter temperatures.
- [ ] **Paper map** of your area, in case your phone and GPS aren't working.
- [ ] If you drive rural roads with no cell coverage, consider a **satellite messenger** that can send an SOS, such as the [Garmin inReach Mini 2](/gear/garmin-inreach-mini-2/).

## If you get stranded

1. **Stay with your car** unless you can see help close by. A car is easier for rescuers to find than a person on foot.
2. **Call or text for help** and give your exact location.
3. **Keep the exhaust pipe clear of snow.** A blocked tailpipe can push carbon monoxide into the cabin. The CDC also says never to run a car in an attached garage, even with the door open. See [carbon monoxide safety during a power outage](/guides/carbon-monoxide-safety-during-an-outage/).
4. **Run the engine about 10 minutes each hour** for heat, as Ready.gov recommends, with a window cracked slightly for fresh air.
5. **Turn on the dome light** while the engine runs so you can be seen, and tie something bright to the antenna or door.
6. **Move your arms and legs** to keep blood flowing, and huddle together with any passengers for warmth.

## Before every winter

- Have your battery tested. Many auto parts stores do it for free.
- Check tire tread and pressure. Pressure drops as temperatures fall.
- Keep your gas tank at least half full all winter.

## Common questions

**If I get stuck, should I leave the car to find help?**
Stay with the car unless you can see help close by. A car is easier for rescuers to find than a person on foot, and it gives you a wind break.

**How often should I run the engine for heat?**
Ready.gov recommends running the engine about 10 minutes each hour, with a window cracked slightly for fresh air, and the exhaust pipe kept clear of snow.

**How do I know whether to drive at all?**
If a Winter Storm Warning or Ice Storm Warning is in effect, the safer plan is to stay home. [Winter Storm Watch vs. Warning](/guides/winter-storm-watch-vs-warning/) explains the alerts, and official conditions are on the [What's happening now](/#live-status) board.
