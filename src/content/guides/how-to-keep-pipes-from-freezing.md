In much of the Deep South, hard freezes don't come often enough for most of us to have a routine. Then a cold snap arrives and everyone's asking the same thing: *do I need to drip my faucets tonight?* This guide covers what to do before a freeze, the night it hits, and if a pipe freezes anyway. It's based on the American Red Cross's frozen-pipe guidance.

## Why pipes burst

The Red Cross explains it plainly: water expands when it freezes, and that expansion puts enough pressure on metal or plastic pipes to break them. A burst pipe often isn't noticed until it thaws and water starts pouring out. That's why a few minutes of prep is worth it.

## Which pipes freeze first

According to the Red Cross, the pipes most likely to freeze are:

- **Pipes exposed to severe cold**, like outdoor hose bibs, swimming pool supply lines, and sprinkler lines.
- **Water supply pipes in unheated interior spaces**, like crawl spaces, basements, attics, garages, and kitchen cabinets.
- **Pipes that run along exterior walls** with little or no insulation.

Lots of Southern homes have plumbing in exactly these places: raised homes on crawl spaces, mobile and manufactured homes, and pipes running through exterior walls or attics. If that sounds like your house, take a freeze warning seriously even if the cold only lasts a night or two.

## Before cold weather: one-time prep

Do these on a mild weekend before the first freeze. Each one comes from the Red Cross's list.

- [ ] **Disconnect, drain, and store garden hoses.** Close the inside valve that feeds each outdoor faucet, then open the outdoor faucet so it can drain. Leave it open so any water left inside has room to expand.
- [ ] **Drain pool and sprinkler supply lines** following the manufacturer's or installer's directions. Don't add antifreeze to these lines unless you're told to.
- [ ] **Insulate exposed pipes** in crawl spaces, attics, garages, and under cabinets. Insulate both hot and cold lines. The Red Cross mentions pipe sleeves and **UL-listed heat tape or heat cable**. Even newspaper offers some protection in areas that don't freeze often.
- [ ] **Add insulation** to attics, basements, and crawl spaces if you can.
- [ ] **Find your main water shut-off valve** and make sure you can turn it. Label it. If a pipe bursts, this is the first thing you'll need, and it's usually in a dark spot. Keep a [headlamp](/gear/black-diamond-spot-400/) nearby.

## The night of a freeze: what to do

When the National Weather Service issues a **Freeze Warning** or a **Hard Freeze** forecast for your area (here's [what each alert means](/guides/winter-storm-watch-vs-warning/)), run through this list:

- [ ] **Drip the cold water** from faucets served by exposed pipes. The Red Cross notes that running water through a pipe, even at a trickle, helps keep it from freezing. If your water utility asks people to limit dripping to protect system pressure, follow its instructions.
- [ ] **Open kitchen and bathroom cabinet doors** under sinks on outside walls so warm room air reaches the pipes. Move cleaners and chemicals up out of reach of kids and pets first.
- [ ] **Keep the garage door closed** if water lines run through the garage.
- [ ] **Keep the thermostat at the same temperature day and night.** The Red Cross says skipping the lower nighttime setting may raise your heating bill but can prevent a much costlier repair.
- [ ] **Going away?** Leave the heat on, set no lower than **55°F**, as the Red Cross recommends.

## If the power goes out during a freeze

No power usually means no heat, and the pipes are at greater risk. A few things still help:

- Keep dripping faucets on exposed lines if you have water pressure.
- Keep cabinet doors open and the house closed up to hold in whatever warmth is left.
- **Don't use a grill, camp stove, gas oven, or generator indoors to warm the house or pipes.** The CDC and Ready.gov both warn about carbon monoxide. If you use an indoor-rated fuel heater, follow the manufacturer's ventilation instructions and run a battery-powered CO alarm, like the [Eton Blackout Buddy CO detector](/gear/eton-blackout-buddy-co/). See [how to stay warm when the power goes out](/guides/how-to-stay-warm-without-power/) for the full rundown.
- If you'll be away from a home that won't have heat for a long stretch, ask a licensed plumber or your water utility whether you should shut off the main and drain the lines.

## How to tell if a pipe is frozen

The Red Cross's tip: **if you turn on a faucet and only a trickle comes out, suspect a frozen pipe.** Likely spots are along exterior walls and where your water line enters the house through the foundation. If one pipe is frozen, check the other faucets too, since others may be frozen as well.

## How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

Following the Red Cross's steps:

1. **Keep the faucet open.** As the ice melts, running water helps melt the rest.
2. **Apply gentle heat** to the frozen section. Use an electric heating pad wrapped around the pipe, an electric hair dryer, a portable space heater kept away from anything flammable, or towels soaked in hot water.
3. **Never use an open flame.** The Red Cross specifically says not to use a blowtorch, kerosene or propane heater, charcoal stove, or other open-flame device.
4. **Keep heating until full water pressure comes back.**
5. **Call a licensed plumber** if you can't find the frozen spot, can't reach it, or can't thaw it.

Watch where you're working. If a pipe has already split, water will come out as it thaws. That's the moment to know where your main shut-off is.

## If a pipe bursts

- **Shut off the main water valve right away.** A [multi-tool](/gear/leatherman-wave-plus/) can help with a stubborn handle, but don't force a corroded valve to the point of breaking it.
- **Turn off electricity to the affected area** only if you can do it safely. Stay out of standing water near outlets or appliances.
- **Call a plumber**, and take photos for your insurance.

## Common questions

**Should I drip faucets during a freeze if the power is already out?**
Yes, if you still have water pressure. A slight drip on pipes along outside walls is the usual official advice from the American Red Cross and the CDC. If you have already lost pressure, do not keep a faucet open, and get ready to shut the main valve if a pipe bursts.

**Is it safe to thaw a frozen pipe with a torch?**
No. The American Red Cross specifically says not to use a blowtorch, kerosene or propane heater, charcoal stove, or other open-flame device. Use gentle heat instead, such as an electric heating pad, a hair dryer, a portable space heater kept away from anything flammable, or towels soaked in hot water, and keep the faucet open. Know where the main shut-off is first.

**When does a Winter Storm Warning or Freeze Warning mean I should start these steps?**
Start the one-time prep before cold weather, and use the night-of-a-freeze steps as soon as a watch or a freeze warning is issued. [Winter Storm Watch vs. Warning](/guides/winter-storm-watch-vs-warning/) explains each alert.
