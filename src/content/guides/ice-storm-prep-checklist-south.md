In Mississippi and across the Deep South, ice is often a bigger problem than snow. The National Weather Service explains that heavy ice can bring down trees and topple utility poles, cutting power and communications for days. Even a thin glaze makes roads and steps dangerous. The good news is that you usually get a day or two of warning. Here's how to use it, hour by hour.

This is our ice storm checklist: a countdown from 48 hours out to the first ice. If the power is already out, go to [what to do when the power goes out in a Mississippi ice storm](/guides/power-outage-ice-storm-mississippi/). For everything in one place, including pipes, pets, cars and cleanup, read the [full winter storm and power outage guide](/guides/winter-storm-power-outage-prep-guide-south/).

## 48 hours out: a Winter Storm Watch is issued

Ready.gov says Winter Storm Watches usually come 12 to 48 hours before a storm. Treat a watch as your shopping-and-charging window. (Not sure what the alerts mean? See [winter storm watch vs. warning](/guides/winter-storm-watch-vs-warning/).)

**Water**

- [ ] Store **one gallon per person per day for several days**, as Ready.gov recommends, for drinking and sanitation. Include pets.
- [ ] On a **well pump**, you'll have no water when the power goes out. Fill extra jugs, and fill a bathtub for flushing.
- [ ] Keep a backup filter, like a [LifeStraw](/gear/lifestraw-personal/), in case of a boil-water notice. Always follow your local water system's instructions.

**Food**

- [ ] Stock several days of food that **needs no cooking or refrigeration**: canned goods (plus a manual can opener), peanut butter, crackers, cereal, and dried fruit.
- [ ] If you keep a longer-term backup like a [Mountain House bucket](/gear/mountain-house-classic-bucket/), remember it needs hot water. Only heat water on a stove that's safe to use, and that means outdoors for camp stoves.
- [ ] Freeze a few water bottles. They help keep the freezer or a cooler cold, and you can drink them as they thaw.

**Medications and medical needs**

- [ ] Refill prescriptions now. Pharmacies may close or lose power.
- [ ] If anyone depends on powered medical equipment or refrigerated medication, Ready.gov suggests talking with your medical provider about an outage plan ahead of time.

**Light, power, and alerts**

- [ ] A **flashlight for every household member**, as Ready.gov recommends, plus extra batteries. Lanterns like the [Goal Zero Lighthouse 600](/gear/goal-zero-lighthouse-600/) light up a whole room.
- [ ] A **battery or hand-crank weather radio**, like the [Midland ER310](/gear/midland-er310-crank-radio/), for NWS alerts if cell service goes down.
- [ ] Charge every phone, power bank, and laptop. If you have a portable energy reserve, like the [Jackery Explorer 1000 Plus](/gear/jackery-explorer-1000-plus/), charge it to full. Not sure if you need one? Read our [energy reserve guide](/guides/how-to-pick-an-energy-reserve/).

**Safety**

- [ ] **Test your smoke and CO alarms.** Ready.gov recommends both, with battery backups. A plug-in CO alarm with no battery won't work in an outage. See the [Eton Blackout Buddy CO detector](/gear/eton-blackout-buddy-co/).
- [ ] If you own a generator, check it now, outside. The CDC says to run it only outdoors, **more than 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents**. Never run it in a garage.
- [ ] If you'll use a fuel-burning heater, make sure the manufacturer rates it for indoor use, read its ventilation instructions, and have fuel stored safely. Compare options in [Home Heating Backup](/home-heating-backup/).

**Car and cash**

- [ ] Fill the gas tank. Ready.gov recommends keeping it full in winter, and pumps need power to run.
- [ ] Get some cash, since card readers go down with the power.
- [ ] Stock your car kit. Ready.gov suggests jumper cables, sand, a flashlight, warm clothes, blankets, bottled water, and non-perishable snacks. A lithium jump starter like the [NOCO Boost GB40](/gear/noco-boost-gb40/) means you don't need a second car. Full list: [winter car emergency kit](/guides/winter-car-emergency-kit/).
- [ ] If you don't own one, get an **ice scraper**. A [snow broom with a scraper](/gear/snow-joe-telescoping-snow-broom/) clears the whole windshield and roof.

## 24 hours out: the forecast firms up

**Protect the pipes** (from the American Red Cross's frozen-pipe guidance):

- [ ] Disconnect and drain garden hoses. Close the inside valve to each outdoor faucet, then open the outdoor faucet to drain it.
- [ ] Insulate exposed pipes in crawl spaces, garages, and attics.
- [ ] Find your main water shut-off and make sure it turns.
- Full details: [how to keep pipes from freezing](/guides/how-to-keep-pipes-from-freezing/).

**Around the house**

- [ ] Bring pets inside, and bring in or tie down anything that could blow around or crack in the cold.
- [ ] If you can, park away from large trees and limbs. Ice-loaded branches can fall during and after the storm.
- [ ] Pick your **warm room**: a small interior room with few windows where everyone can gather if the heat goes out. Gather blankets, sleeping bags, and warm layers there. See [how to stay warm when the power goes out](/guides/how-to-stay-warm-without-power/).
- [ ] Put slip-on **ice cleats** like [Yaktrax Pro](/gear/yaktrax-pro/) by the door for checking on animals or the mailbox.
- [ ] Do laundry and run the dishwasher while you still have power.

**Write things down**

- [ ] Your power company's outage number (Entergy Mississippi, Mississippi Power, your electric power association, or whoever serves you), your water utility, your county emergency management office, and family contacts. Keep it on paper.

## 12 hours out: a Warning is issued

Ready.gov says Winter Storm Warnings usually come 12 to 24 hours ahead, and that if you're under one, you should find shelter right away.

- [ ] Finish errands and get home. Ready.gov says to stay off roads if at all possible during a winter storm.
- [ ] Set faucets on exposed pipes to drip if a hard freeze is expected, and open the cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls.
- [ ] Keep the thermostat steady day and night. The Red Cross recommends skipping the nighttime setback during a freeze.
- [ ] Put flashlights, headlamps, and the radio where you can find them in the dark.
- [ ] Top off all charging one last time.

## When the ice arrives

Once the storm starts, switch to our [what to do when the power goes out in an ice storm](/guides/power-outage-ice-storm-mississippi/) guide. The key rules:

- **No grills, camp stoves, generators, or gas ovens for heat indoors.** The CDC and Ready.gov both warn about carbon monoxide, which the CDC calls odorless and colorless.
- **Keep the fridge and freezer closed.** Per Ready.gov, food stays cold about 4 hours in a closed fridge and about 48 hours in a full freezer.
- **Treat downed lines as live**, and stay far from anything they touch, including fences and puddles. Report them to the utility. Limbs and lines can still fall as ice melts or the wind shifts.
- **Stay off icy steps and walks if you can.** The CDC notes that many winter injuries come from falls on ice. If you must go out, wear shoes with traction, take small steps, and use handrails.
- **Skip outdoor cleanup while ice is still falling.** Ready.gov warns that overexertion in winter weather raises the risk of a heart attack.
- **Check on neighbors** who are older, have medical needs, or live alone.

## Common questions

**What's the difference between a Winter Storm Watch and a Warning?**
A watch means conditions are favorable and you still have a prep window. A warning means hazardous winter weather is expected or happening and you should finish prep and stay put. See [Winter Storm Watch vs. Warning](/guides/winter-storm-watch-vs-warning/).

**How do I lower carbon monoxide risk if we lose power?**
Keep generators outdoors and more than 20 feet from the home, and never use a grill, camp stove, or oven to heat indoors. Details are in [Carbon Monoxide Safety During a Power Outage](/guides/carbon-monoxide-safety-during-an-outage/).

**Where can I check my utility's outage map?**
Use the provider named on your bill. Official utility and cooperative maps for Mississippi counties are linked from the [Mississippi local resources](/local/mississippi/) pages.
